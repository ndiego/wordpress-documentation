# What is WordPress?

After reading this page you can explain what WordPress is, what kinds of sites people build with it, and which way of getting WordPress fits you.

## WordPress in one paragraph

WordPress is free, open source software for building and running websites. You install it on a web server (or a hosting company does it for you), and from then on you manage the whole site in your web browser: you write and publish content, change how the site looks, and add features, without writing code. A large share of all websites on the internet run on WordPress, from personal blogs to the sites of newspapers, universities and governments.

## What kind of software it is

People describe WordPress in several ways, and each is true.

- **A publishing tool.** WordPress started in 2003 as blogging software, and writing and publishing is still at its center. You write in the block editor, where every paragraph, image and heading is a block you can move and style.
- **A content management system (CMS).** WordPress stores your content (posts, pages, images, comments) separately from its design. You can change the design and keep every word you wrote.
- **A platform.** Thousands of themes change how a site looks, and thousands of plugins add features: online stores, forms, memberships, multilingual sites, course platforms. Developers build on WordPress through its APIs, including a REST API that other applications can use.

## What you can build

A WordPress site can be:

- A blog or a personal site.
- A website for a business, a club, a school or a nonprofit.
- A portfolio for photos, art or writing.
- An online store, with a commerce plugin.
- A news site or magazine with many authors.
- A documentation site, like this one.

You start with a theme that gives the site its layout, then add pages and posts. As your needs grow, you add plugins rather than starting over.

## Two ways to get WordPress

The name "WordPress" covers two different things, and the difference matters when you choose.

**WordPress, the software** (often called WordPress.org or self-hosted WordPress). You download it from [WordPress.org](https://wordpress.org/download/) and run it on a web server you rent from a hosting provider, or on your own computer. You control everything: which themes and plugins you install, your data and your backups. In return, you are responsible for updates and security, though many hosts take care of much of that work.

**WordPress.com**, a hosting service run by the company Automattic. It runs the same WordPress software and handles the server, updates and security for you. Which themes and plugins you can install depends on the plan you choose.

Many other hosting companies also offer WordPress with the setup and maintenance done for you. [Choose where to run WordPress](choose-where-to-run-wordpress.md) compares the options, and [WordPress.org and WordPress.com](wordpress-org-and-wordpress-com.md) explains the relationship between the two names in more detail.

This documentation describes the WordPress software. Almost everything in it also applies to WordPress.com and to managed hosts, but some hosts change or hide parts of the dashboard. If a screen looks different on your site, your host's own help pages explain why.

## What "open source" means for you

WordPress is released under the GNU General Public License (GPL), which means you can use it for any purpose, study and change its code, and share it, at no cost and without asking anyone. Your site is not locked to one company: you can move it, with all its content, to any host that runs WordPress.

WordPress is built by a community of volunteers and contributors from companies around the world. [The WordPress project](the-wordpress-project.md) tells that story.
